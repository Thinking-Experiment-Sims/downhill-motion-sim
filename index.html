💡 Key Finding: The second-degree coefficient A in the position equation is exactly half the slope of the velocity equation: A = ½ · a.

General Kinematics Equations

The general models for any object undergoing constant acceleration:
x(t) = ½ · a · t² + v0 · t + x0
v(t) = a · t + v0
Because acceleration is constant downhill, velocity increases linearly with time, which causes position to grow quadratically with the square of time (t²).

📝 Analysis Questions Helper (Click each question to view guided reasoning)

4. What is the physical meaning of the slope in the velocity vs. time graph? ▾
Slope = Acceleration (a). The slope of a velocity vs. time graph represents the rate at which the cart's velocity changes each second (measured in m/s² or cm/s²). Because the line is straight, the acceleration is constant.
5. What is the physical meaning of the y-intercept in the velocity vs. time graph? ▾
y-intercept = Initial Velocity (v0). The y-intercept represents the cart's velocity at clock reading t = 0 s. When released from rest, this value is approximately 0 m/s.
7. Compare the second-degree coefficient with the slope of the velocity equation. ▾
Relationship: The second-degree coefficient A in the position equation is equal to half the slope of the velocity equation (A = ½ · a). This confirms why the kinematic formula contains the factor of ½.
8. Compare the first-degree coefficient with the y-intercept of the velocity equation. ▾
Relationship: The first-degree coefficient B in the position equation is equal to the y-intercept of the velocity equation, which corresponds to the initial velocity v0 of the cart.
9. What is the physical meaning of the y-intercept in the position equation? ▾
y-intercept = Initial Position (x0). The y-intercept C represents the location of the cart along the track at the start of timing (t = 0 s).
